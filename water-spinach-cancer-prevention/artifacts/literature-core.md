1. **Bjeldanes & Chang (1977)** — Bjeldanes, L. F.; Chang, G. W. (1977). Mutagenic activity of quercetin and related compounds. *Science* 197:577-8.  
   https://doi.org/10.1126/science.327550 · PMID 327550  
   Summary: In Ames tests, quercetin was mutagenic (more so with metabolic activation), whereas dihydroquercetin (taxifolin), rutin and the other flavonoids tested were not.  
   Role: Distinguishes the water-spinach aglycone (taxifolin) from quercetin on genotoxicity, a point in its favour and a reason not to treat the two as interchangeable.

2. **ATBC Cancer Prevention Study Group (1994)** — Alpha-Tocopherol, Beta Carotene Cancer Prevention Study Group (1994). The effect of vitamin E and beta carotene on the incidence of lung cancer and other cancers in male smokers. *N Engl J Med* 330:1029-35.  
   https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199404143301501 · PMID 8127329  
   Summary: In 29,133 male smokers, β-carotene supplements increased lung-cancer incidence (by about 18\%) instead of lowering it.  
   Role: The central cautionary tale for SG3: an "antioxidant from vegetables" given as a supplement caused harm, so food-level hypotheses must not be turned into pills.

3. **Hollman et al. (1995)** — Hollman, P. C.; de Vries, J. H.; van Leeuwen, S. D.; Mengelers, M. J.; Katan, M. B. (1995). Absorption of dietary quercetin glycosides and quercetin in healthy ileostomy volunteers. *Am J Clin Nutr* 62:1276-82.  
   https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/62.6.1276 · PMID 7491892  
   Summary: In nine ileostomy volunteers, 52\% of quercetin glucosides from onions were absorbed, against 24\% of pure quercetin aglycone and 17\% of rutinoside.  
   Role: Establishes that glucose conjugation *enhances* human small-intestinal uptake, the first link from a glucoside-bearing vegetable to systemic exposure (E2).

4. **Omenn et al. (1996)** — Omenn, G. S.; Goodman, G. E.; Thornquist, M. D.; Balmes, J.; Cullen, M. R.; Glass, A.; et al. (1996). Effects of a combination of beta carotene and vitamin A on lung cancer and cardiovascular disease. *N Engl J Med* 334:1150-5.  
   https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199605023341802 · PMID 8602180  
   Summary: In CARET, β-carotene plus vitamin A raised lung-cancer incidence (RR 1.28) and mortality in high-risk adults.  
   Role: A second harmful antioxidant prevention trial, reinforcing that SG3 concerns whole foods, not isolated compounds.

5. **Woo et al. (1997)** — Woo, J.; Leung, S. S.; Lam, C. W.; Ho, S. C.; Lam, T. H.; Janus, E. D. (1997). Plasma total antioxidant capacity in an adult Hong Kong Chinese population. *Clin Biochem* 30:553-7.  
   https://doi.org/10.1016/s0009-9120(97)00112-4 · PMID 9399024  
   Summary: In 728 Hong Kong Chinese adults, those who ate water spinach twice or more a week had higher mean plasma total antioxidant capacity.  
   Role: The only human biomarker datum for water spinach we found. It hints that eating it changes blood chemistry, but it is cross-sectional and unrelated to cancer.

6. **Day et al. (1998)** — Day, A. J.; DuPont, M. S.; Ridley, S.; Rhodes, M.; Rhodes, M. J.; Morgan, M. R.; et al. (1998). Deglycosylation of flavonoid and isoflavonoid glycosides by human small intestine and liver beta-glucosidase activity. *FEBS Lett* 436:71-5.  
   https://doi.org/10.1016/s0014-5793(98)01101-6 · PMID 9771896  
   Summary: Cell-free extracts of human small intestine and liver rapidly deglycosylated quercetin 4′-glucoside and several flavonoid 7-glucosides, but left quercetin 3-glucoside, 3,4′-diglucoside and rutinosides unchanged.  
   Role: Shows that human CBG cuts 7-*O*-glucosides (M2), and that it has substrate limits, including at the 3-position, which bear directly on the diglucoside.

7. **Kasturi et al. (1998)** — Kasturi, V. K.; Dearing, M. P.; Piscitelli, S. C.; Russell, E. K.; Sladek, G. G.; O'Neil, K.; et al. (1998). Phase I study of a five-day dose schedule of 4-Ipomeanol in patients with non-small cell lung cancer. *Clin Cancer Res* 4:2095-102.  
   No DOI assigned — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9748125/ · PMID 9748125  
   Summary: In a phase I trial of intravenous 4-ipomeanol in 44 lung-cancer patients, the dose-limiting toxicity was hepatic and there were no objective responses.  
   Role: A cautionary node for SG3: a toxin named for *Ipomoea* that looked promising pre-clinically failed in humans; cytotoxicity is not cure.

8. **Day et al. (2000)** — Day, A. J.; Cañada, F. J.; Díaz, J. C.; Kroon, P. A.; Mclauchlan, R.; Faulds, C. B.; et al. (2000). Dietary flavonoid and isoflavone glycosides are hydrolysed by the lactase site of lactase phlorizin hydrolase. *FEBS Lett* 468:166-70.  
   https://doi.org/10.1016/s0014-5793(00)01211-4 · PMID 10692580  
   Summary: Lactase-phlorizin hydrolase purified from sheep small intestine hydrolysed quercetin-4′-glucoside, quercetin-3-glucoside and isoflavone glucosides.  
   Role: Supplies the luminal enzyme for M2 and, because it is a sheep enzyme, grounds the mammalian homology used in SG2.

9. **Braune et al. (2001)** — Braune, A.; Gütschow, M.; Engst, W.; Blaut, M. (2001). Degradation of quercetin and luteolin by Eubacterium ramulus. *Appl Environ Microbiol* 67:5558-67.  
   https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.67.12.5558-5567.2001 · PMID 11722907  
   Summary: The human gut anaerobe *Eubacterium ramulus* degraded quercetin via taxifolin to 3,4-dihydroxyphenylacetic acid, and converted added taxifolin the same way.  
   Role: Gut bacteria can both free and destroy taxifolin (M3). The bacterial route around the α-link may therefore deliver phenolic acids rather than taxifolin.

10. **Graczyk et al. (2001)** — Graczyk, T. K.; Gilman, R. H.; Fried, B. (2001). Fasciolopsiasis: is it a controllable food-borne disease?. *Parasitol Res* 87:80-3.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s004360000299 · PMID 11199855  
   Summary: The giant intestinal fluke *Fasciolopsis buski* is transmitted by eating raw aquatic plants grown in contaminated ponds.  
   Role: A safety limit: raw water greens carry parasite risk, so any dietary recommendation must specify cooking.

11. **G\"othberg et al. (2002)** — Göthberg, A.; Greger, M.; Bengtsson, B. E. (2002). Accumulation of heavy metals in water spinach (Ipomoea aquatica) cultivated in the Bangkok region, Thailand. *Environ Toxicol Chem* 21:1934-9.  
   https://doi.org/10.1002/etc.5620210922 · PMID 12206434  
   Summary: Water spinach grown around Bangkok accumulated mercury, cadmium and lead, especially near polluted water.  
   Role: A safety limit: contaminant load depends on the growing water and could offset any benefit.

12. **Knekt et al. (2002)** — Knekt, P.; Kumpulainen, J.; Järvinen, R.; Rissanen, H.; Heliövaara, M.; Reunanen, A.; et al. (2002). Flavonoid intake and risk of chronic diseases. *Am J Clin Nutr* 76:560-8.  
   https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/76.3.560 · PMID 12198000  
   Summary: In 10,054 Finnish adults, men with higher quercetin intake had lower lung-cancer incidence (RR 0.42, highest vs lowest quartile).  
   Role: An early human node (E4) linking dietary flavonols to lower cancer risk; associational.

13. **Berrin et al. (2003)** — Berrin, J. G.; Czjzek, M.; Kroon, P. A.; McLauchlan, W. R.; Puigserver, A.; Williamson, G.; et al. (2003). Substrate (aglycone) specificity of human cytosolic beta-glucosidase. *Biochem J* 373:41-8.  
   https://doi.org/10.1042/BJ20021876 · PMID 12667141  
   Summary: A homology model and mutagenesis of human CBG showed that its activity on flavonoid glucosides depends on the aglycone, the sugar and the linkage, and identified aglycone-binding residues.  
   Role: Gives the structural basis for testing whether CBG accepts the diglucoside (M2), an open gap.

14. **Day et al. (2003)** — Day, A. J.; Gee, J. M.; DuPont, M. S.; Johnson, I. T.; Williamson, G. (2003). Absorption of quercetin-3-glucoside and quercetin-4'-glucoside in the rat small intestine: the role of lactase phlorizin hydrolase and the sodium-dependent glucose transporter. *Biochem Pharmacol* 65:1199-206.  
   https://doi.org/10.1016/s0006-2952(03)00039-x · PMID 12663055  
   Summary: In rat everted jejunum, quercetin-3-glucoside was absorbed only after LPH hydrolysis.  
   Role: Shows the rodent gut uses the same enzyme route as human tissue, so rodent feeding data can bear on humans (SG2).

15. **Malalavidhane et al. (2003)** — Malalavidhane, T. S.; Wickramasinghe, S. M.; Perera, M. S.; Jansz, E. R. (2003). Oral hypoglycaemic activity of Ipomoea aquatica in streptozotocin-induced, diabetic wistar rats and Type II diabetics. *Phytother Res* 17:1098-100.  
   https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.1345 · PMID 14595595  
   Summary: Shredded *I. aquatica* leaves lowered fasting glucose in diabetic rats, and a single blended dose lowered 2-h glucose in type 2 diabetic patients.  
   Role: Shows that the eaten plant has measurable biological effects in rats and people. The effect is not related to cancer, but it makes a feeding study feasible.

16. **N\'emeth et al. (2003)** — Németh, K.; Plumb, G. W.; Berrin, J. G.; Juge, N.; Jacob, R.; Naim, H. Y.; et al. (2003). Deglycosylation by small intestinal epithelial cell beta-glucosidases is a critical step in the absorption and metabolism of dietary flavonoid glycosides in humans. *Eur J Nutr* 42:29-42.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s00394-003-0397-3 · PMID 12594539  
   Summary: LPH and CBG were isolated from human small-intestinal mucosa and hydrolysed flavonoid glycosides, with large inter-individual variation.  
   Role: Confirms both enzymes in human tissue (M2); the variation implies any benefit would differ between people.

17. **Hernandez et al. (2004)** — Hernandez, B. Y.; McDuffie, K.; Franke, A. A.; Killeen, J.; Goodman, M. T. (2004). Reports: plasma and dietary phytoestrogens and risk of premalignant lesions of the cervix. *Nutr Cancer* 49:109-24.  
   https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327914nc4902_1 · PMID 15489203  
   Summary: In an exploratory Honolulu case-control study (122 cases, 183 controls), a lignan-source food group listed as "taro leaves/ong choy/marunggay" was *positively* associated with premalignant cervical lesions.  
   Role: The only human cancer-related association involving water spinach that we found, and it points the wrong way. It is weak and grouped, but it is disclosed as counter-evidence.

18. **Kroon et al. (2004)** — Kroon, P. A.; Clifford, M. N.; Crozier, A.; Day, A. J.; Donovan, J. L.; Manach, C.; et al. (2004). How should we assess the effects of exposure to dietary polyphenols in vitro?. *Am J Clin Nutr* 80:15-21.  
   https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/80.1.15 · PMID 15213022  
   Summary: Argued that *in vitro* polyphenol studies should test the conjugates found in blood, at concentrations of about 0.1–10 µmol/L reached after a polyphenol-rich meal.  
   Role: The methodological core of SG1: most cell studies cited here used aglycones at far higher concentrations.

19. **Thu et al. (2004)** — Thu, N. N.; Sakurai, C.; Uto, H.; Van Chuyen, N.; Lien, D. T.; Yamamoto, S.; et al. (2004). The polyphenol content and antioxidant activities of the main edible vegetables in northern Vietnam. *J Nutr Sci Vitaminol (Tokyo)* 50:203-10.  
   https://doi.org/10.3177/jnsv.50.203 · PMID 15386933  
   Summary: Water spinach supplied about 45\% of a mean polyphenol intake of 595 mg catechin equivalents per day in northern Vietnam.  
   Role: Anchors dose realism for SG1: high consumers take in hundreds of milligrams of polyphenols a day, much of it from this plant.

20. **Manach et al. (2005)** — Manach, C.; Williamson, G.; Morand, C.; Scalbert, A.; Rémésy, C. (2005). Bioavailability and bioefficacy of polyphenols in humans. I. Review of 97 bioavailability studies. *Am J Clin Nutr* 81:230S-242S.  
   https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/81.1.230S · PMID 15640486  
   Summary: Across 97 human bioavailability studies, total plasma polyphenol metabolites ranged from 0 to 4 µmol/L after about 50 mg aglycone equivalents.  
   Role: The quantitative anchor for SG1 and M5: realistic plasma levels are low-micromolar at most and consist of conjugates.

21. **Prasad et al. (2005)** — Prasad, K. N.; Divakar, S.; Shivamurthy, G. R.; Aradhya, S. M. (2005). Isolation of a free radical-scavenging antioxidant from water spinach (Ipomoea aquatica Forsk). *Journal of the Science of Food and Agriculture* 85:1461-1468.  
   https://doi.org/10.1002/jsfa.2125  
   Summary: Activity-guided fractionation of *I. aquatica* leaves yielded an antioxidant (DPPH EC50 83 µg/mL) tentatively identified as \DQG.  
   Role: The isolation paper for the key compound (M1). Its α-linkage at C-3 is the basis of the α-link caveat (M3).

22. **Prasad et al. (2005b)** — Prasad, K.; Ashok, G.; Raghu, C.; Shivamurthy, G.; Vijayan, P.; Aradhya, S. (2005). In vitro cytotoxic properties of Ipomoea aquatica leaf. *Indian Journal of Pharmacology* 37:397.  
   https://doi.org/10.4103/0253-7613.19079  
   Summary: The purified diglucoside, a crude leaf extract and a fraction were tested on Vero (normal), HEp-2 and A-549 cells. The pure compound's CTC$_{50}$ values were 387, 156 and 394 (reported as mg/mL), and the crude extract was more potent.  
   Role: This is the "2005 weak *in vitro*" study of the thread. The compound is weakly and non-selectively cytotoxic, so direct cell killing by the parent glycoside is not a plausible dietary mechanism.

23. **Cao et al. (2007)** — Cao, S.; Norris, A.; Wisse, J. H.; Miller, J. S.; Evans, R.; Kingston, D. G. (2007). Ipomoeassin F, a new cytotoxic macrocyclic glycoresin from the leaves of Ipomoea squamosa from the Suriname rainforest. *Nat Prod Res* 21:872-6.  
   https://doi.org/10.1080/14786410600929576 · PMID 17680496  
   Summary: Ipomoeassin F, a resin glycoside from *I. squamosa* leaves, inhibited A2780 ovarian cancer cells with an IC50 of 0.036 µM.  
   Role: The genus makes potent cytotoxins; these are pharmacological, not dietary, and lie outside the chain.

24. **Jin et al. (2007)** — Jin, Y. R.; Lee, M. S.; Lee, J. H.; Hsu, H. K.; Lu, J. Y.; Chao, S. S.; et al. (2007). Intake of vitamin A-rich foods and lung cancer risk in Taiwan: with special reference to garland chrysanthemum and sweet potato leaf consumption. *Asia Pac J Clin Nutr* 16:477-88.  
   No DOI assigned — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17704030/ · PMID 17704030  
   Summary: In a Taiwanese case-control study (301 cases, 1,204 controls), the highest tertile of sweet-potato-leaf intake was associated with lower lung-cancer risk (adjusted OR 0.43–0.65).  
   Role: The closest human cancer datum for an edible *Ipomoea* leaf. It concerns a sister species and is case-control, but it points the thesis's way (E4).

25. **Kottra & Daniel (2007)** — Kottra, G.; Daniel, H. (2007). Flavonoid glycosides are not transported by the human Na+/glucose transporter when expressed in Xenopus laevis oocytes, but effectively inhibit electrogenic glucose uptake. *J Pharmacol Exp Ther* 322:829-35.  
   https://doi.org/10.1124/jpet.107.124040 · PMID 17495124  
   Summary: Flavonoid glycosides were not transported by the human sodium-glucose transporter SGLT1 expressed in oocytes.  
   Role: Removes the intact-uptake route, leaving deglycosylation (M2, M3) as the main path to absorption.

26. **Lee et al. (2007)** — Lee, S. B.; Cha, K. H.; Selenge, D.; Solongo, A.; Nho, C. W. (2007). The chemopreventive effect of taxifolin is exerted through ARE-dependent gene regulation. *Biol Pharm Bull* 30:1074-9.  
   https://doi.org/10.1248/bpb.30.1074 · PMID 17541156  
   Summary: Taxifolin induced quinone reductase in HCT116 colon cells with low cytotoxicity and, at 60 µM, activated antioxidant-response-element genes such as *NQO1* and *GSTM1*.  
   Role: The mechanistic node for M7: taxifolin behaves as a phase-II-inducing chemopreventive, which fits prevention better than cytotoxicity.

27. **Neufert et al. (2007)** — Neufert, C.; Becker, C.; Neurath, M. F. (2007). An inducible mouse model of colon carcinogenesis for the analysis of sporadic and inflammation-driven tumor progression. *Nat Protoc* 2:1998-2004.  
   https://doi.org/10.1038/nprot.2007.279 · PMID 17703211  
   Summary: A protocol for azoxymethane (AOM) colon carcinogenesis in mice, alone (tumours within about 30 weeks) or with dextran sodium sulfate (DSS; multiple tumours within about 10 weeks), with tumour scoring systems.  
   Role: The methods basis of the proposed animal feeding study (Section \ref{sec:animal}).

28. **Vuong et al. (2007)** — Vuong, T. A.; Nguyen, T. T.; Klank, L. T.; Phung, D. C.; Dalsgaard, A. (2007). Faecal and protozoan parasite contamination of water spinach (Ipomoea aquatica) cultivated in urban wastewater in Phnom Penh, Cambodia. *Trop Med Int Health* 12 Suppl 2:73-81.  
   https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-3156.2007.01944.x · PMID 18005318  
   Summary: Water spinach grown in Phnom Penh wastewater lakes was heavily contaminated with faecal bacteria and protozoan parasites.  
   Role: A safety limit on wastewater-grown produce.

29. **Bobe et al. (2008)** — Bobe, G.; Sansbury, L. B.; Albert, P. S.; Cross, A. J.; Kahle, L.; Ashby, J.; et al. (2008). Dietary flavonoids and colorectal adenoma recurrence in the Polyp Prevention Trial. *Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev* 17:1344-53.  
   https://doi.org/10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-07-0747 · PMID 18559549  
   Summary: In the Polyp Prevention Trial cohort, high flavonol intake was associated with lower recurrence of advanced colorectal adenoma (OR 0.24).  
   Role: A human signal for flavonols and a pre-cancerous endpoint (E4), from a secondary analysis.

30. **Egert et al. (2008)** — Egert, S.; Wolffram, S.; Bosy-Westphal, A.; Boesch-Saadatmandi, C.; Wagner, A. E.; Frank, J.; et al. (2008). Daily quercetin supplementation dose-dependently increases plasma quercetin concentrations in healthy humans. *J Nutr* 138:1615-21.  
   https://doi.org/10.1093/jn/138.9.1615 · PMID 18716159  
   Summary: Quercetin at 50–150 mg/day for two weeks raised plasma quercetin dose-dependently (median peak 431 nmol/L at 150 mg) without changing oxidative or inflammatory markers.  
   Role: Even supplement-level flavonol intake gives sub-micromolar plasma levels (SG1).

31. **Luo et al. (2008)** — Luo, H.; Jiang, B. H.; King, S. M.; Chen, Y. C. (2008). Inhibition of cell growth and VEGF expression in ovarian cancer cells by flavonoids. *Nutr Cancer* 60:800-9.  
   https://doi.org/10.1080/01635580802100851 · PMID 19005980  
   Summary: Of 12 compounds tested on OVCAR-3 cells, taxifolin, quercetin and others inhibited growth, but taxifolin was the weakest VEGF inhibitor, attributed to its missing C2=C3 double bond.  
   Role: Grok-cited and verified. Dihydroquercetin is active but not equivalent to quercetin.

32. **Marcussen et al. (2008)** — Marcussen, H.; Joergensen, K.; Holm, P. E.; Brocca, D.; Simmons, R. W.; Dalsgaard, A. (2008). Element contents and food safety of water spinach (Ipomoea aquatica Forssk.) cultivated with wastewater in Hanoi, Vietnam. *Environ Monit Assess* 139:77-91.  
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s10661-007-9817-9 · PMID 17593534  
   Summary: Element contents of wastewater-grown water spinach in Hanoi were mostly below food-safety limits.  
   Role: A partly reassuring counterpoint: contamination is site-specific, not inherent.

33. **Takachi et al. (2008)** — Takachi, R.; Inoue, M.; Ishihara, J.; Kurahashi, N.; Iwasaki, M.; Sasazuki, S.; et al. (2008). Fruit and vegetable intake and risk of total cancer and cardiovascular disease: Japan Public Health Center-Based Prospective Study. *Am J Epidemiol* 167:59-70.  
   https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwm263 · PMID 17928402  
   Summary: In 77,891 Japanese adults, vegetable intake was not associated with total cancer (HR 0.94, 95\% CI 0.84–1.05).  
   Role: Null Asian cohort evidence for vegetables and total cancer, tempering E4.

34. **Wang et al. (2009)** — Wang, L.; Lee, I. M.; Zhang, S. M.; Blumberg, J. B.; Buring, J. E.; Sesso, H. D. (2009). Dietary intake of selected flavonols, flavones, and flavonoid-rich foods and risk of cancer in middle-aged and older women. *Am J Clin Nutr* 89:905-12.  
   https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.2008.26913 · PMID 19158208  
   Summary: In 38,408 women, intake of flavonols and flavones was not associated with total or site-specific cancer.  
   Role: A large null cohort for flavonols (counter-evidence to E4).

35. **Murota et al. (2010)** — Murota, K.; Matsuda, N.; Kashino, Y.; Fujikura, Y.; Nakamura, T.; Kato, Y.; et al. (2010). alpha-Oligoglucosylation of a sugar moiety enhances the bioavailability of quercetin glucosides in humans. *Arch Biochem Biophys* 501:91-7.  
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.abb.2010.06.036 · PMID 20638359  
   Summary: Adding α-linked glucose units to the sugar of quercetin 3-glucoside increased its bioavailability in humans.  
   Role: Human evidence that α-glucosyl sugars on a flavonoid glucoside do not block absorption, which softens, but does not settle, the α-link caveat (M3).

36. **Karna et al. (2011)** — Karna, P.; Gundala, S. R.; Gupta, M. V.; Shamsi, S. A.; Pace, R. D.; Yates, C.; et al. (2011). Polyphenol-rich sweet potato greens extract inhibits proliferation and induces apoptosis in prostate cancer cells in vitro and in vivo. *Carcinogenesis* 32:1872-80.  
   https://doi.org/10.1093/carcin/bgr215 · PMID 21948980  
   Summary: A polyphenol-rich sweet-potato greens extract inhibited prostate-cancer cells and, orally at 400 mg/kg, reduced PC-3 xenograft growth by about 69\% in mice.  
   Role: An oral anticancer effect of an *Ipomoea* leaf extract, at a dose far above diet (SG1) and in a treatment model (SG3).

37. **Klein et al. (2011)** — Klein, E. A.; Thompson, I. M.; Tangen, C. M.; Crowley, J. J.; Lucia, M. S.; Goodman, P. J.; et al. (2011). Vitamin E and the risk of prostate cancer: the Selenium and Vitamin E Cancer Prevention Trial (SELECT). *JAMA* 306:1549-56.  
   https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2011.1437 · PMID 21990298  
   Summary: In SELECT, vitamin E supplements increased prostate-cancer risk (HR 1.17).  
   Role: Another antioxidant-supplement harm, bounding SG3.

38. **Bjelakovic et al. (2012)** — Bjelakovic, G.; Nikolova, D.; Gluud, L. L.; Simonetti, R. G.; Gluud, C. (2012). Antioxidant supplements for prevention of mortality in healthy participants and patients with various diseases. *Cochrane Database Syst Rev* 2012:CD007176.  
   https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007176.pub2 · PMID 22419320  
   Summary: A Cochrane review found that antioxidant supplements do not reduce mortality, and some increase it.  
   Role: High-level cautionary evidence against extrapolating from "antioxidant" to benefit (M9).

39. **Jin et al. (2012)** — Jin, H.; Leng, Q.; Li, C. (2012). Dietary flavonoid for preventing colorectal neoplasms. *Cochrane Database Syst Rev* 2012:CD009350.  
   https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009350.pub2 · PMID 22895989  
   Summary: A Cochrane review found insufficient and conflicting evidence that dietary flavonoids prevent colorectal neoplasms.  
   Role: A high-level inconclusive verdict limiting E4.

40. **Meira et al. (2012)** — Meira, M.; da Silva, E. P.; David, J. M.; David, J. P. (2012). Review of the genus Ipomoea: traditional uses, chemistry and biological activities. *Rev Bras Farmacogn* 22:682-713.  
   https://doi.org/10.1590/s0102-695x2012005000025  
   Summary: A review of the genus *Ipomoea*: traditional uses, chemistry and biology, including the 2005 water-spinach cytotoxicity data.  
   Role: The review the first author quoted on 24 July 2026. It reports the C-3 sugar as β and the CTC$_{50}$ values in mg/mL.

41. **Oi et al. (2012)** — Oi, N.; Chen, H.; Ok Kim, M.; Lubet, R. A.; Bode, A. M.; Dong, Z. (2012). Taxifolin suppresses UV-induced skin carcinogenesis by targeting EGFR and PI3K. *Cancer Prev Res (Phila)* 5:1103-14.  
   https://doi.org/10.1158/1940-6207.CAPR-11-0397 · PMID 22805054  
   Summary: Taxifolin bound and inhibited EGFR and PI3K and, applied topically, suppressed UV-induced skin tumours in mice.  
   Role: The model evidence for M6, by a topical rather than dietary route (SG1, SG2).

42. **Zheng et al. (2012)** — Zheng, S. Y.; Li, Y.; Jiang, D.; Zhao, J.; Ge, J. F. (2012). Anticancer effect and apoptosis induction by quercetin in the human lung cancer cell line A-549. *Mol Med Rep* 5:822-6.  
   https://doi.org/10.3892/mmr.2011.726 · PMID 22200874  
   Summary: Quercetin inhibited A549 cells (IC50 about 1.0–1.4 µmol/L) and, at 8 mg/kg, A549 xenografts in mice.  
   Role: Grok-cited and verified; one of the few potencies near the diet-achievable range.

43. **Deng et al. (2013)** — Deng, X. H.; Song, H. Y.; Zhou, Y. F.; Yuan, G. Y.; Zheng, F. J. (2013). Effects of quercetin on the proliferation of breast cancer cells and expression of survivin in vitro. *Exp Ther Med* 6:1155-1158.  
   https://doi.org/10.3892/etm.2013.1285 · PMID 24223637  
   Summary: Quercetin inhibited MCF-7 proliferation, induced G0/G1 arrest and apoptosis, and reduced survivin.
